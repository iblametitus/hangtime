The problem

01 / 05

Your feed is
a training plan
nobody checked.

Every day athletes see new jump drills on TikTok and Instagram, usually with a promise attached: “I added 6 inches to my vertical.” Some of these drills are great. Some are filler. From the clip alone, nobody can tell which.

And even a good drill can hurt when it lands in the wrong place: a third plyometric session in a week, a reactive drill on top of a heavy squat day. The question is never only “does it work?” but also “does it work for me, and what does it replace?”

{# ---------- principles + swap ---------- #}

Design principles

04 / 05

The model understands.
The algorithm decides.

No quote without proof

Models invent citations. Hangtime only lets a quote through if it appears verbatim in the study card or search result it claims to come from.

Direct vs. indirect evidence

Injury prevention or tendon studies can back a long-term benefit, never a promise like “+10 cm in 4 weeks”. Every card carries that label.

Same input, same swap

Changing someone's training plan is a decision, not a vibe. The swap is plain, unit-tested code that the model may explain but never overrule.

Fair to creators

Hangtime never calls a video a lie. It says what the drill is good for and what it can realistically do on its own.
